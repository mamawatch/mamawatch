Vision

A world where no woman dies from preventable pregnancy-related complications — achieved through empowered communities, stronger health systems and the early detection, timely referral and effective management of preeclampsia and eclampsia.

Mission

To reduce preventable maternal and newborn deaths through awareness, education, advocacy, community engagement, health-system strengthening and strategic partnerships.

Why MamaWatch exists

A Survivor's Story. A Community's Watch. A Mission.

Stella's pregnancy seemed normal until persistent headaches, swelling and fatigue were repeatedly dismissed as malaria or stress. By 28 weeks her condition had become critical — and it was a neighbor, not a clinician, who recognized the danger signs and insisted she return to hospital. At 34 weeks she underwent an emergency Caesarean section, fighting severe preeclampsia for nearly ten hours. Her daughter was born prematurely at 1.3kg and spent weeks in Kangaroo Mother Care before reaching a healthy weight.

Recovering, Stella asked herself one question: "What if that woman had not recognized the warning signs?" She found that most women around her had never heard the word preeclampsia, despite knowing the symptoms all too well. That realization — that one informed neighbor had saved her life — became the foundation of MamaWatch.

"I survived preeclampsia, but many women do not get a second chance. Every woman deserves the opportunity to survive pregnancy and watch her child grow." — Stella
"I survived because someone from the community was watching. MamaWatch exists to make sure more women have someone watching."
Our approach

Community knowledge is part of maternal health care.

MamaWatch works where community awareness, family support and clinical care meet — anchored by a network of trained Community Watchers who help families recognize danger signs and act early. The goal is simple: help people recognize danger, ask questions and support timely access to care.

01 · RECOGNIZE

Recognize Early

Helping women, families and communities recognize pregnancy danger signs early and understand when symptoms require medical attention.

02 · ACT

Speak Up & Act Early

Encouraging women and those around them to take concerning symptoms seriously and seek appropriate medical care without unnecessary delay.

03 · WATCH

Community Watchers

Training trusted community members to recognize warning signs, speak up, encourage timely referral and watch over mothers without replacing healthcare professionals.

04 · EXTEND

Extend Learning

Using community education, survivor experiences, digital engagement and peer support to keep maternal-health knowledge moving beyond individual outreach sessions.

Beyond outreach events

Turning health education into practical action.

At the Salama Muslim Women’s Community, participants reported earlier and more consistent antenatal care, shared warning signs with other women, and referred four women to MamaWatch. At Bongkisheri, two men connected their wives for counselling and hospital referral; both women were preeclampsia survivors and were followed up through care. Following the Presbyterian CWF session, a woman who recognized symptoms of postpartum preeclampsia was referred to Ngaoundéré Regional Hospital and followed until her blood pressure returned to normal.

Continuous ANC education

Bamenda Regional Hospital

MamaWatch delivered a two-week volunteer initiative in the ANC unit, reaching 105 women with education on preeclampsia and eclampsia, danger signs, risk factors and when to seek urgent care. Watcher educational cards were distributed for use at home. The midwife reported improved regularity in ANC attendance, earlier reporting of concerning symptoms and stronger communication between women and health workers.

Governance & accountability

Governance & Accountability

MamaWatch Association (MWA) is officially registered under Receipt of Declaration N° 030/H.52/AB/SAKJP/RM, issued 23 April 2026 by the Prefect of Vina Division, Adamawa Region. Strategic governance is kept separate from day-to-day implementation, with a clear feedback loop from community outreach back to leadership and partners.

Monitoring cadence: outreach data is reviewed monthly by the M&E focal point, consolidated quarterly against targets, assessed semi-annually with the Board and partners, and evaluated annually to shape the coming year's plan.

Sustainability: built on three pillars — diversified funding from grants and donors, strong local ownership through community volunteers and survivor networks, and formal collaboration with regional health authorities so awareness and screening become part of the existing health system.

Governance: MamaWatch is led by an executive committee headed by President and Founder Nkanjoh Stella Kari, alongside Technical Adviser Tchoffo Mekontso Olivier, Medical Lead Mbua Besingi Essono and Secretary General Ngeugang Donfo Sonia; a formal Board of Directors and Advisory Committee are being constituted to strengthen oversight as the association grows.

Health-system coordination: MamaWatch is formalizing Memoranda of Understanding with the Regional Delegation of Public Health for Adamawa and referral hospitals such as Ngaoundéré Regional Hospital, aligning training and screening with national maternal-health guidelines.
